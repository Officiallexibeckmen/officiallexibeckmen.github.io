How it works

The whole method is that nothing is vague. If you can't check it, we haven't finished writing it down.

  1. Everything is priced in public before it's asked for

    We find the supplier, check the goods are actually in stock and purchasable, and publish the case size, the unit price and the link. The arithmetic sits on the page so you can redo it.

  2. People fund the specific thing they care about

    Supplies are bought as whole cases, so an item is either funded or it isn't. You can cover the whole thing or five dollars of it, and progress is live — your contribution shows up in the number the moment it lands.

  3. Funded things get bought, in list order, and closed out

    Once an item reaches its price it stops taking money and gets ordered. The list is worked from the top down. If a contribution overshoots the last few dollars, it moves to the next unfunded item — or you can ask for it back. You choose which at checkout.

  4. Finished work stays up

    Closed drives and events keep their pages, with what was bought and what it cost. The record is the point — it's how you tell whether the last one actually happened.

Things worth knowing

Roots Worth Tending is not a registered 501(c)(3). It's a small volunteer effort, not an incorporated charity. Contributions are not tax-deductible, and you shouldn't claim them as such.

Nobody here is paid. There's no office and no overhead — a contribution buys the thing it was given for, at the price shown, from the supplier named.

That's also why the prices are all linked. You don't have to trust the arithmetic; you can check it in about thirty seconds, and if a supplier's price has moved since a page was built, the link will show you that too.

If something here doesn't add up, say so and it gets fixed.
